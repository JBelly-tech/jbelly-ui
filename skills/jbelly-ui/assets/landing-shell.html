02 · Windows

A window you promised is a constraint, not a note

Every stop carries the window the customer agreed to, the service time your driver actually needs, and the hour the shop closes for lunch. Mishwar plans inside all three and says which promises cannot be kept before a van leaves the yard.

  • Service time is learned per customer from the last twenty deliveries.
  • Breaks and shift ends are hard limits, not suggestions.
  • Conflicts arrive as a short call list with the two nearest free slots.

Questions from the first call

Asked by every dispatcher we meet

Something else? Write to sales@mishwar.app and a person answers.

Do we have to replace our ERP?

No. Mishwar reads a CSV export, or a REST endpoint if you have one, and writes the finished plan back the same way. Three of our largest customers still run the system they bought in 2011.

What happens when a driver leaves the planned sequence?

Nothing breaks. The remaining stops re-sequence on the phone, the office sees the new arrival times within a minute, and the change is written to the day log under the driver name.

Can two depots share vans?

Yes. A van belongs to a depot but can be lent for a day. The plan, the kilometres and the payroll export follow the van, not the depot.

Is our customer data ours to take away?

It is. One button exports every stop, address and delivery record as CSV, and account data is deleted thirty days after you ask.

Does the driver app work in Arabic?

Yes, right to left, with Arabic addresses and Arabic refusal reasons. Nine depots on Mishwar run the office in English and the phones in Arabic.

How long until the first real plan?

A depot with a clean order export is planning the same day. The slow part is agreeing service times per customer; Mishwar starts with a default and learns from the deliveries.

Start with a single depot and one day of orders. If the plan is not better than the one on the whiteboard, stop there — there is nothing to cancel.
